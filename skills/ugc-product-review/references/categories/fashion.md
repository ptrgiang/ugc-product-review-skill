## High-interaction-risk try-on rule

Putting on outerwear can overload a video model because it combines multiple limbs, multiple sleeves/openings, flexible cloth, shoulder rotation, occlusion, contact, and product-geometry continuity at once.

For jackets, coats, cardigans, and similar garments:
- do not show the complete rapid dressing motion unless it is essential to the concept;
- prefer a hard cut from `held` to `partially worn`;
- start with one arm already fully inside the correct sleeve and that shoulder seam already seated;
- show only the second arm sliding continuously through the remaining sleeve;
- keep the sleeve visibly attached to the correct shoulder seam;
- preserve sleeve length, quilting/panel layout, zipper alignment, collar, hem, and garment scale throughout;
- after insertion, use one small settling action such as a light downward tug on the front panels;
- keep camera movement minimal during the fragile interaction.

Preferred continuity:
`held → hard cut → one arm already inserted → second arm enters sleeve → both arms inserted → garment settles → worn/styled`

Failure signs:
- garment teleports or snaps onto the torso;
- sleeve merges with the arm;
- sleeve swaps sides, detaches, or changes length;
- shoulder seam moves independently of the garment;
- hand appears outside the physical sleeve path;
- quilt/panel geometry changes while being worn;
- both arms and garment state change at the same instant.

If the dressing action is not itself the hero proof, prioritize fit/silhouette reliability over showing every intermediate dressing step.
